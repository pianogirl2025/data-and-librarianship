'In the eye of the beholder': Knowledge and skills requirements for data professionals

The article explores the emerging and evolving knowledge and skills requirements for data professionals working in various organizational contexts such as universities, scientific research institutions, business, and government. Through interviews with 36 data professionals and their employers, this study identifies that data roles are diverse and often not clearly defined, with roles ranging from data librarians and managers to data scientists and analysts.

Key findings reveal that while technical skills in data management, curation, analytics, and programming are important, interpersonal skills such as communication, adaptability, curiosity, and teamwork are critical across all sectors. Domain-specific knowledge varies by organization type. Scientific knowledge is vital in research settings, while business acumen and regulatory understanding are crucial in government and corporate environments. Emphasizing that data work is typically done by multidisciplinary teams rather than individuals, highlighting the importance of collaboration. Training pathways are diverse and many data professionals learn on the job, although formal education in data-related skills is becoming increasingly necessary.

Finally, the article suggests that as data roles mature, clearer role definitions and tailored educational programs will better prepare future data professionals, bridging gaps between organizational needs and professional competencies.

Kennan, M. A. (2017). 'In the eye of the beholder': Knowledge and skills requirements for data professionals. Information Research, 22(4).

Assessing the value of a fellowship program and community of practice at the intersection of data science and library and information science

Goldman and Mitchell (2024) investigate the value of the LEADING (LIS Education and Data Science Integrated Network Group) fellowship program and its associated community of practice (CoP) designed to bridge the disciplines of data science and library & information science (LIS). Using a sequential mixed‑methods design, the authors apply the Value Creation Framework (VCF) with five stages that are immediate, potential, applied, realised, and transformative, along with a focus on diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) to understand participant engagement and perceived outcomes.

Key findings include strong evidence of connectedness, emotional engagement, and shared community values as drivers of perceived value in the fellowship/CoP. Participants reported gains not only in technical skills related to DS/LIS intersections such as analytics and data curation, but also in soft skills like networking, mentorship, and peer learning. The fellowship/CoP model allowed participants to move from initial exposure (immediate) toward applying skills and realising benefits in practice, and some even reported transformative changes in identity as data‑librarians.

The authors emphasise that such programs help fill gaps in training where formal LIS or data science education may not yet fully cover interdisciplinary needs, particularly in library contexts. The study highlights that communities of practice alongside fellowship experiences can magnify learning, provide long‑term support, and foster cultural change in institutions regarding data‑skills integration. In conclusion, Goldman and Mitchell suggest that well‑structured fellowship programs coupled with active communities of practice offer a promising pathway to prepare future professionals at the data science and LIS intersection, supporting both individual growth and institutional capacity building.

Goldman, C., & Mitchell, E. T. (2024). Assessing the value of a fellowship program and community of practice at the intersection of data science and library and information science. portal: Libraries & the Academy, 24(4), 807-846.

Data science in data librarianship: Core competencies of a data librarian

Semeler, Pinto, and Rozados (2019) examine the core competencies needed by data librarians who operate at the intersection of data science and traditional librarianship. Drawing on a literature review and interviews with practicing data librarians, the authors identify both technical and non-technical skill sets that are increasingly essential.

Key findings include:

  • Technical Skills: Statistical analysis, programming, data cleaning, modeling, and workflow tools.
  • Information Governance: Metadata, data curation, documentation, and long-term preservation.
  • Domain Knowledge: Understanding disciplinary data practices and ethical/legal issues.
  • Soft Skills: Communication, teaching, project management, and user support.
  • Adaptability: Staying current with quickly evolving data science methods.

The authors call for LIS education to integrate data science fundamentals so future data librarians are prepared for hybrid, interdisciplinary roles.

Semeler, A. R., Pinto, A. L., & Rozados, H. B. F. (2019). Data science in data librarianship: Core competencies of a data librarian. Journal of Librarianship & Information Science, 51(3), 757–770. https://doi.org/10.1177/0961000618813542

The Data Science and Digital Scholarship Fellowship Program (DS²F)

Senseney and Oliver (2025) present the DS²F program, a library-based initiative addressing gaps in graduate-level data-intensive training and digital scholarship. The program offers a structured fellowship where graduate students receive training in data management, digital tools, pedagogy, and open educational resources.

Fellows then transition into instructional roles, delivering campus workshops and peer-focused training. This model enhances digital scholarship literacy and builds institutional capacity.

Successes include increased student engagement and strengthened library–graduate student collaboration, while challenges include managing fellows’ academic workloads and securing long-term funding.

Senseney, M., & Oliver, J. C. (2025). The Data Science and Digital Scholarship Fellowship Program (DS²F): A library-based model for addressing curricular gaps in data-intensive training and digital pedagogy. College & Research Libraries. Advance online publication. https://doi.org/10.5860/crl.XXXX
