About Me

My name is Amy Aguirre, and I am currently pursuing my Master of Library and Information Science (MLIS) at San José State University. This LibGuide was created as part of my work in INFO 210: Reference, and it represents my ongoing exploration of the evolving relationship between data science and librarianship.

I come from a background in performing arts and education. I earned my Bachelor of Arts in Ethnomusicology with a minor in Education from the University of California, Davis, where I studied music, culture, pedagogy, and the ways communities use music to express identity and experience. Additionally, I hold Associate of Arts degrees in Spanish for Heritage Speakers, Language Arts, Humanities, and Music, all of which have shaped my interdisciplinary approach to learning and research.

My academic and professional interests sit at the intersection of music, culture, education, technology, and librarianship. I am fascinated by how emerging technologies, especially data science, AI, and digital research tools can support more equitable, meaningful, and culturally responsive library services.

As an information professional in training, I am committed to exploring how librarians can use data-driven approaches to strengthen instruction, enhance reference services, and support student learning. I am particularly interested in the ethical use of data in educational and library environments, especially as it relates to privacy, accessibility, and community-centered practice.

By combining my background in the arts with my growing technical skills, I hope to contribute to projects that bridge creativity and computation. Whether through digital scholarship, metadata work, community archives, or instructional design, I believe libraries are uniquely positioned to use data to empower users while honoring their stories and identities.
